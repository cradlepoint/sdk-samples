DSDS WAN Verify

When a SIM's link fails, waiting on someone to notice and drive to the site costs hours. DSDS WAN Verify tests the SIM that's carrying traffic every couple of seconds, and the instant a failure is confirmed it switches to the other SIM in about 30 seconds — hands-off, unattended. It helps that a Dual-SIM modem reports live signal for both SIMs at once, so the app can even fail back to your preferred SIM the moment it recovers.

Catches what "connected" hides

Ping, HTTP, and a signal floor on the SIM carrying traffic — so a slot that still claims "connected" but has a dead link gets caught and acted on.

Sees signal on both SIMs

How it decides

Every couple of seconds the app checks the SIM that's actually carrying traffic. These are the rules that keep it decisive without making it flap.

Reacts in seconds, switches in ~30

It reads the connected SIM's results every 2 seconds. Each test confirms its own failure through a quick retry, so a confirmed failure is acted on right away — then the SIM switch itself takes about 30 seconds. No waiting on a human.

Tests the live SIM three ways

Ping (via the router's IP Verify), an HTTP GET/HEAD, and a minimum signal level. Each is per-SIM and optional; ping and HTTP combine as both must pass or either passes.

Reads the standby SIM's signal

Connectivity can only be tested on the connected SIM, but a DSDS modem keeps reporting live signal for the standby SIM too. That lets the app check a destination before committing — and know when a recovered SIM is worth returning to, so it can fail back to your preferred SIM on its own.

Decisive without flapping

A short settle window after each switch and a confirm-before-acting rule keep it from bouncing. A signal failover needs no holdoff (the level is always measurable); the 1-hour holdoff is reserved for a connectivity failover, where the standby link can't be tested.
